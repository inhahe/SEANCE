Recording Audio

Home Graph Basics Recording Automation

You can sing, play, or talk into your computer while the song is playing, and SEANCE will keep it. The take turns into a normal block of audio sitting on the timeline, so from then on it just plays along with everything else — and it saves with your project.

You can also record several inputs at the same time — two mics, or a mic and a guitar — and each one lands on its own separate track instead of being mushed together.

Recording audio vs. recording automation. This page is about capturing sound coming into your computer. If you want to capture knob moves you make during playback, that's a different feature — see Recording Automation.

Record one microphone

  1. Move the playhead to where you want the take to start.
  2. Press Play & Record.
  3. Perform. When you're done, press Stop.

That's genuinely it. When you press record, SEANCE looks at what's plugged into your sound device and builds a track for anything that doesn't have one yet — named after the input, dropped into a free spot on the canvas, and already wired to your Output node. A single USB mic gives you a track called something like Mic (C615).

A red block appears on that track, starting exactly where the playhead was when you hit record. Rewind, press Play, and you'll hear it back with the rest of the song. Save the project and it'll still be there next time you open it.

Press record again later and the same track is re-used — you don't accumulate a pile of near-identical tracks for the one microphone. Plug in a second mic, though, and a second track appears for it on the next record press.

Prefer to do it by hand? Turn off Settings → Auto-Create Tracks for Audio Inputs. Then nothing is created for you, and only tracks you built and armed yourself are ever recorded into: add an Audio Track node, set its Input Channel value row (0 = the first input on your sound device), right-click → Record Here to arm it, and wire its Audio output to your Output node.
Hear yourself while you play. Turn on the track's Input Monitor toggle and your live input is piped to the speakers as you perform, using that track's own Volume and Pan. Use headphones — monitoring through speakers with a mic open will howl.

Record several inputs at once

There's no special "multitrack mode" to switch on, and nothing extra to do. Plug in both mics (or a mic and a guitar), press Play & Record once, and each input gets its own track:

  • Mic 1 (Focusrite) and Line In 2 (Focusrite), say — the name tells you which physical socket the sound came from, so you don't have to remember which track is which.
  • Both record at the same time, but into separate blocks of audio. That's what you want: you can now set the two levels independently, or put an effect on only one of them.

There's no limit on how many at once; the real limit is how many inputs your sound device actually has. A plain laptop or a USB mic usually has one. A 2-input audio interface gives you two.

Hearing (or not hearing) the track you're recording into

While you're recording onto a track, that track is silenced — if there was already a take on it, you won't hear the old one playing underneath the one you're performing. Everything else in the song keeps playing as normal, so you still have something to play along to.

If you'd rather hear the old take as you record over it — handy for doubling a part, or checking you're in time with it — turn on Settings → Play Tracks Back While Recording Them. You can flip it mid-take and it changes straight away.

Hearing yourself is a separate thing. The setting above is about the old audio already on the track. To hear your live input as you perform, use the track's Input Monitor toggle — and wear headphones, or an open mic near the speakers will howl.

After the take

  • Don't like it? Ctrl+Z removes the whole take in one step.
  • The track disarms itself when you stop, so pressing plain Play can't record over what you just captured. Pressing Play & Record re-arms it for another pass (or right-click → Record Here if you've turned auto-create off).
  • It's a normal audio block now. Drag it, trim it, slide the audio inside it, add effects after the track, bounce it — everything that works on imported audio works on your recording.
  • The actual file is a 24-bit WAV in a recordings folder next to the SEANCE program, named after the track and the time you recorded it.

If something goes wrong

Nothing was recorded. The most common cause is that the track wasn't armed — check for the Record Here mark before you press Play & Record. After that, check that the Input Channel matches a real input, and that your microphone is selected as the input device in the audio settings.

"Recording dropped audio" popped up. Your drive couldn't keep up and some of the take is missing. Recording to a faster drive, or arming fewer tracks at once, fixes it. SEANCE tells you when this happens rather than quietly handing you a take with holes in it — so if you don't see this message, nothing was lost.

How long can a take be? As long as you like. There's no time limit — only free space on your drive.
