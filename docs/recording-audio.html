Recording Audio

Home Graph Basics Recording Automation

You can sing, play, or talk into your computer while the song is playing, and SEANCE will keep it. The take turns into a normal block of audio sitting on the timeline, so from then on it just plays along with everything else — and it saves with your project.

You can also record several inputs at the same time — two mics, or a mic and a guitar — and each one lands on its own separate track instead of being mushed together.

Recording audio vs. recording automation. This page is about capturing sound coming into your computer. If you want to capture knob moves you make during playback, that's a different feature — see Recording Automation.

Record one microphone

  1. Right-click empty canvas → add an Audio Track node. This is the track your recording will land on.
  2. On that node, find the Input Channel value row and set it to 0. That means "the first input on my sound device" — for a single plugged-in mic, that's the one you want.
  3. Right-click the node → Record Here. The track is now armed.
  4. Wire the track's Audio output to your Output node, so you'll hear it back.
  5. Move the playhead to where you want the take to start, then press Play & Record.
  6. Perform. When you're done, press Stop.

A red block appears on the track, starting exactly where the playhead was when you hit record. Rewind, press Play, and you'll hear it back with the rest of the song. Save the project and it'll still be there next time you open it.

Hear yourself while you play. Turn on the track's Input Monitor toggle and your live input is piped to the speakers as you perform, using that track's own Volume and Pan. Use headphones — monitoring through speakers with a mic open will howl.

Record several inputs at once

There's no special "multitrack mode" to switch on. Just arm more than one track:

  1. Make one Audio Track per input you want to capture.
  2. Give each one a different Input Channel — 0 for the first input on your device, 1 for the second, and so on.
  3. Right-click each → Record Here.
  4. Press Play & Record once. Every armed track records at the same time.

On Stop you get one separate block of audio per track, which is what you want — you can now set the levels of the two mics independently, or put an effect on only one of them.

There's no limit on how many you can arm; the real limit is how many inputs your sound device actually has. A plain laptop or a USB mic usually has one (channel 0). A 2-input audio interface gives you channels 0 and 1.

You make the tracks yourself. SEANCE doesn't look at your sound device and automatically build a track for every input it finds. That's deliberate — a track is only ever recorded into if you explicitly armed it, so an accidental Play can't overwrite something you wanted to keep.

After the take

  • Don't like it? Ctrl+Z removes the whole take in one step.
  • The track disarms itself when you stop, so pressing Play again can't record over what you just captured. Right-click → Record Here again when you're ready for another pass.
  • It's a normal audio block now. Drag it, trim it, slide the audio inside it, add effects after the track, bounce it — everything that works on imported audio works on your recording.
  • The actual file is a 24-bit WAV in a recordings folder next to the SEANCE program, named after the track and the time you recorded it.

If something goes wrong

Nothing was recorded. The most common cause is that the track wasn't armed — check for the Record Here mark before you press Play & Record. After that, check that the Input Channel matches a real input, and that your microphone is selected as the input device in the audio settings.

"Recording dropped audio" popped up. Your drive couldn't keep up and some of the take is missing. Recording to a faster drive, or arming fewer tracks at once, fixes it. SEANCE tells you when this happens rather than quietly handing you a take with holes in it — so if you don't see this message, nothing was lost.

How long can a take be? As long as you like. There's no time limit — only free space on your drive.
